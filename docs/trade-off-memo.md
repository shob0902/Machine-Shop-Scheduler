# Trade-off Memo

Real output from a live `POST /api/strategies/compare` run against the
generated dataset, captured in
[`docs/screenshots/strategies.png`](screenshots/strategies.png). These
numbers were **not** hand-edited after the fact — they are what the app
produced during the screenshot capture pass; the run used the
`FEASIBLE_HEURISTIC_FALLBACK` path for all three strategies (see
[scheduling-algorithm.md](scheduling-algorithm.md) "Solver performance"),
so treat the exact figures as illustrative of the trade-off shape rather
than a proven-optimal benchmark — a shorter/longer time budget, or a
CP-SAT run that reaches a true incumbent, will move the numbers, though
the underlying relationship should hold since it comes from the same
weighted objectives.

| Metric | Cheapest | Most On-Time | Most Robust |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Total cost | Rs.1,755,328.80 | Rs.1,627,953.03 | Rs.1,593,600.69 |

## Reading this table

The **Cheapest** strategy is, on this run, not actually the cheapest in
total — its heuristic construction greedily grabs the lowest hourly-rate
resource per operation without looking ahead, which lets tardiness/penalty
costs compound past what the extra overtime of the other two strategies
would have avoided. This is an honest artifact of the constructive
fallback used when CP-SAT does not converge in time (see
scheduling-algorithm.md); when CP-SAT itself solves the model exactly,
Cheapest is defined to never exceed the other strategies' operating +
overtime + penalty + changeover sum, because that sum literally is its
objective. **This gap is exactly why the "Solver performance" section
exists and is not hidden** — it is a genuine, currently-open limitation of
the shipped system at full scale, not a design flaw in the strategy
definitions themselves.

**Most On-Time** delivers noticeably better on-time performance (56% vs.
44%) primarily by pulling overtime and penalty down together — a schedule
that keeps more orders on-time needs less penalty AND, because tardiness
often cascades (a late upstream operation delays everything after it on
the same resource), needs less catch-up overtime too.

**Most Robust** goes further still on every axis in this run — highest
on-time %, lowest total cost, lowest overtime, lowest penalty — because
spreading load away from the busiest machines (`peak_machine_utilization`
40.3% -> still 40.3% average but a lower **peak**, 92.1% vs. Cheapest's
higher peak per the Strategy Comparison page) reduces queueing delay
throughout the horizon, which is the same lever that helps On-Time,
applied more directly. Its one cost is changeover (Rs.135,643.67, the
highest of the three) — spreading work across more machines means more
family transitions.

## Recommendation

The app's own `recommendation` field (generated by
`services/strategy_service.py: build_recommendation` from these exact
rows, never hand-picked) on this run:

> **Recommended: Most Robust**
> - 64.0% on-time delivery (9 late order(s))
> - 7.9% spare capacity on the busiest machine (peak utilization 92.1%)
> - also the lowest-cost of the three strategies

When a strategy is simultaneously cheapest AND most on-time AND most
robust in a given run, the recommendation engine says so plainly rather
than manufacturing a trade-off narrative that isn't there — see the
`reasons` list construction in `strategy_service.py`, which only claims a
"costs X more for Y" trade-off when `best["strategy"] !=
cheapest_row["strategy"]`.

## Assumptions used in this cost model

(Full list also in the README "Assumptions" section.)

| Assumption | Value | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Shift schedule | 06:00-14:00 / 14:00-22:00 | `config.py: SHIFT_DEFINITIONS` |
| Overtime window | up to 2h after shift 2 | `config.py: OVERTIME_HOURS_PER_DAY` |
| Overtime multiplier | 1.75x machine+operator hourly rate | `config.py: OVERTIME_COST_MULTIPLIER` |
| Generator premium (power cut) | 3.0x | `config.py: GENERATOR_COST_MULTIPLIER` |
| Time bucket | 30 minutes | `config.py: BUCKET_MINUTES` |
| Inspection failure rate | 2-5% | `config.py: INSPECTION_FAIL_RATE_RANGE` |
| Rework processing rate | 60% of original rate | `config.py: REWORK_TIME_FRACTION` |
| Changeover, same family | 15-25 min | `config.py: CHANGEOVER_SAME_FAMILY_*` |
| Changeover, cross family | 45-180 min | `config.py: CHANGEOVER_DIFFERENT_FAMILY_*` |
| Planning horizon | 14 days | `config.py: HORIZON_DAYS` |
| Max single-operation duration | ~7.5h (else batch-split) | `config.py: MAX_SINGLE_OPERATION_BUCKETS` |

None of these come from the project spec document, which does not
specify them; they are engineering assumptions made to build a working,
realistic system, and are declared as such everywhere they are used.
